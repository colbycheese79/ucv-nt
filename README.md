A light linguistic and textual revision of the New Testament derived directly from Noah Webster's 1833 edition of the Bible (*The Holy Bible... in the Common Version*).

---

## About the Base Text & Title

When Noah Webster published his revision of the King James Bible in 1833, he did not title it after himself. The original title page read:

> *The Holy Bible, Containing the Old and New Testaments, in the Common Version. With Amendments of the Language.*

In the 19th century, "the Common Version" was the standard phrase used to designate the traditional 1611 Authorized Version (KJV). Webster's objective was not to produce an entirely new translation, but to clear away obsolete vocabulary and grammatical hurdles while preserving the familiar cadence, reverence, and dignity of the historic text.

This project adopts the title **Updated Common Version New Testament** (UCV-NT) to honor that lineage, signaling a continued effort to maintain a clear, accurate, and readable formal-equivalence English text.

---

## Scope & Translation Philosophy

This revision is strictly limited to the **New Testament**. The philosophy guiding this edition rests on minimal, targeted intrusion:

1. **Preserve the Traditional Cadence:** Retain the formal-equivalence character and majestic rhythm of the KJV/Webster tradition where the meaning remains clear.
2. **Modernize Obsolete Vocabulary:** Update words whose meanings have shifted dramatically or cause misunderstandings for contemporary readers.
3. **Correct Historic Textual Anomalies:** Restore manuscript readings in specific passages where early modern printings or conjectures deviated from the early Greek text.

## Summary of Revisions

### 1. Restoration of "Charity" to "Love"
The archaic usage of "charity" (from the Latin *caritas*) in place of "love" (*agape*) has been updated in **all 28 instances across 26 New Testament passages**:

* **Romans 14:15**
* **1 Corinthians 8:1; 13:1, 2, 3, 4 (3x), 8, 13; 14:1; 16:14**
* **Colossians 3:14**
* **1 Thessalonians 3:6**
* **2 Thessalonians 1:3**
* **1 Timothy 1:5; 2:15; 4:12**
* **2 Timothy 2:22; 3:10**
* **Titus 2:2**
* **Philemon 1:5, 7**
* **1 Peter 4:8; 5:14**
* **2 Peter 1:7**
* **3 John 1:6**
* **Jude 1:12**
* **Revelation 2:19**

### 2. Systematic Vocabulary Modernizations
* **"Haply" $\rightarrow$ "Perhaps":** Changed in all occurrences (e.g., Luke 14:29, Acts 5:39, Acts 17:27) to prevent common confusion with "happily."
* **"Dissimulation" $\rightarrow$ "Hypocrisy":** Updated in passages like **Romans 12:9** and **Galatians 2:13**.
* **Passage-Specific Clarifications:**
  * **Matthew 10:35** – Modernized archaic relational/familial terminology.
  * **Romans 11:14** – Clarified archaic phrasing ("emulation" / "my flesh" $\rightarrow$ "jealousy" / "my own people").
  * **Galatians 5:20** – Modernized obscure terms in the list of the works of the flesh ("emulations", "variance", "seditions" $\rightarrow$ "jealousy", "strife", "divisions").

### 3. Textual Refinements in Revelation
The Book of Revelation includes four targeted textual corrections where early printed English editions drifted from the manuscript tradition:

| Passage | Reading in Earlier Printings | Revision in UCV-NT | Textual Note / Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rev 14:1** | "...having his Father's name written..." | "...having **his name and** his Father's name written..." | Restores the Lamb's name alongside the Father's name based on manuscript evidence. |
| **Rev 16:5** | "...and shalt be..." | "...**O Holy One**..." | Undoes Theodore Beza's 1598 conjectural emendation, restoring the Greek manuscript reading ($\kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\ \delta\acute{o}\sigma\iota o\varsigma$). |
| **Rev 17:8** | "...and yet is..." | "...and **shall be present**..." | Corrects the translation to align with the underlying Greek verb form ($\kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\ \pi\alpha\rho\acute{\epsilon}\sigma\tau\alpha\iota$). |
| **Rev 22:19**| "...book of life..." | "...**tree of life**..." | Corrects Erasmus's historic Latin-to-Greek back-translation printing error ($\xi\acute{\upsilon}\lambda o\upsilon\ \tau\tilde{\eta}\varsigma\ \zeta \omega\tilde{\eta}\varsigma$). |
